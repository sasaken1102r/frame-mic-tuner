# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Auto gain** switch next to **Echo cancel** on the Fine-tune tab. SteamOS's echo cancellation (WebRTC) has automatic gain control built in, so your voice reaches apps about 6 dB louder on the Frame's speakers (echo cancellation on) than on earphones or a USB-C dongle (echo cancellation off), and apps like VRChat need a different mic volume for each. With Auto gain off, the app uses its own copy of SteamOS's main echo canceller (the same `aec.args`) with `webrtc.gain_control2` turned off, so echo cancellation stays on for the Frame's speakers and the voice level matches earphones (within 0.6 dB, measured on a Frame). It runs in the existing `frame-mic-tuner-denoise.service` process, as the smart filters `frame-mic-tuner-ec-source` / `frame-mic-tuner-ec-sink`; PipeWire and WirePlumber are not restarted and `/etc` is not touched (WebRTC's settings can't be changed on a running echo canceller, hence the second one). The tracker puts in either SteamOS's echo canceller or this one, never both, and falls back to SteamOS's as soon as the service's node goes away, so echo cancellation is never lost.
- Auto gain only exists inside echo cancellation, so while Echo cancel is off it is greyed out and shown as off (nothing adjusts the level then); turning Echo cancel back on brings back the remembered value. Both presets keep it on, as in SteamOS; **Frame speakers** on the Quick tab matches only with Auto gain on. It is remembered for each output (`auto_gain` in `config.json`, on when missing), and the output list and voice check recordings say "auto gain off" when it was off.
- The **Signal path** shows Auto gain as a small tag hanging under the echo cancellation step (not beside it, since left to right is the order the sound goes through): filled while SteamOS's echo canceller (with auto gain) is in the path, dashed while the one without auto gain is in use or echo cancellation is off. On the Fine-tune tab, a line under the Echo cancel and Auto gain switches says Auto gain is built into Echo cancel, so it only works while Echo cancel is on.
- New WirePlumber setting `frame-mic.auto-gain` (default true). Restart the headset once after installing; until then Auto gain is greyed out and the bottom line says to restart the Frame. If Auto gain is off and the service isn't running, the bottom line says so in red (SteamOS's echo canceller is used meanwhile).
- `install.sh` now starts `frame-mic-tuner-denoise.service` only once the running WirePlumber knows `frame-mic.auto-gain` (before, `frame-mic.steady-noise`), because a tracker from before this change doesn't know the new nodes. Coming from 0.4.0, Steady noise is therefore off until that restart.
- `--set-gain on|off`; `--print` shows the setting. `--dump-png` has `--fake-gain on|off` and `--fake-gain-missing`, and `--fake-error write-gain`.

## 0.4.0 (2026-11-22)

- **Steady noise**, a new noise filter that keeps breath and whispers. Valve's noise suppression (RNNoise with a voice gate) removes everything that doesn't sound like a voice, including breath and whispers, even at 1 % / 50 ms. Measured on a Frame (test sentences, synthesized whispers and breaths played from a PC speaker, an air purifier on max plus a circulator; raw mic noise about −60 dBFS): Valve's suppressor took breaths down by 10–55 dB, while Steady noise lowered the steady noise by about 20 dB at its default strength of 20 dB (26 dB at 25) and breaths and whispers lost 0–1.4 dB. It subtracts a noise estimate per frequency (the minimum over the last ~3 s, allowed to rise by at most 3 dB per second, so a note held for a long time isn't taken for noise: a held note with vibrato lost 0.7 dB after 8 s, against 20 dB with the plain minimum; the cost is that when the noise gets louder, it takes a few seconds longer to catch up; after silence below −100 dBFS, such as SteamOS muting the mic while the headset isn't worn, it catches up at once, so the noise is gone within about 4 s of putting the headset on), with a Wiener-style gain whose floor is the strength, plus an 80 Hz high-pass; latency is about 21 ms (1024-sample frames). On the **Fine-tune** tab, Noise filter now picks one of three: **Off**, **Voice only** (Valve's) or **Steady noise**, which has its own **Strength** slider (0–40 dB, **Default** = 20 dB) and is remembered for each output like the other settings (`steady_noise` / `steady_noise_db` in the settings file). The **Quick** presets are unchanged (noise filter off).
- Checked through the real mic path after a headset restart (raw mic → EQ → Steady noise → apps, the same test): steady noise −60 → −78 dBFS, breaths −0.4 to −1.2 dB, whispers −0.1 to −0.6 dB; with **Voice only**, one of the three breaths dropped by 43 dB. By ear, whispers sound right at the default 20 dB. Soft breathing is easiest to hear at about 3 dB: from about 5 dB up it gets harder to tell apart from a fan, which is also steady, broadband noise, so lower the strength if you want breathing to come through.
- How it runs: a LADSPA plugin (`~/.local/lib/frame-mic-tuner/frame-mic-denoise.so`) in its own small PipeWire process (`frame-mic-tuner-denoise.service`, `pipewire -c ~/.local/share/frame-mic-tuner/frame-mic-denoise.conf`), inserted as a smart filter after EQ, echo cancellation and Valve's suppressor; PipeWire and WirePlumber are not restarted. On/off is the new WirePlumber setting `frame-mic.steady-noise`; the strength is set live with `pw-cli set-param`. WirePlumber only places a newly added smart filter on the app side if the existing filters list it in `filter.smart.after`, so the tracker adds the filter's name there in the `filters` metadata (Valve's files are untouched). The node is marked `steamos.mic_filter`, so apps (Steam's mic list) don't see it. If the service stops, the filter just leaves the path and the mic keeps working, unfiltered. `--uninstall` removes the service and the plugin.
- Restart the headset once after installing (the WirePlumber script changed). Until then **Steady noise** is greyed out and the panel says to restart the Frame; if the service isn't running, the panel says that too. `install.sh` only enables the service and starts it once the running WirePlumber has the new script, because before the restart the old script (or SteamOS's own tracker) could put the filter into the mic unasked; after the restart it starts with PipeWire.
- `--set-steady on|off` and `--set-steady-db N` (applied at once, the strength isn't saved); `--print` shows the setting, its strength and the node. `--dump-png` has `--fake-steady`, `--fake-steady-db`, `--fake-steady-missing`, `--fake-denoise-stopped` and `--preview-drag-steady`.

## 0.3.6 (2026-11-14)

- **Stop cut-off** for USB-C earphone adapters. Some adapters switch their amplifier off after about a second of digital silence, so the first moment of the next sound (about 0.15 s) is lost. Measured on a Frame with an AB13X adapter, by recording its headphone output on a PC: it isn't WirePlumber suspending the device (playing zeros doesn't help), and an inaudible 5 Hz tone (about −60 dBFS) keeps the amplifier on. With **Stop cut-off** on for the current output, the app keeps playing that tone to it while the app runs (an audio stream named "Frame Mic Tuner keep-awake"; it follows the default output and stops when you switch to an output with it off). It's in **Output**, on every row except the Frame's speakers, and remembered for each output. It's on for AB13X adapters and off for every other output, since other adapters may not need it and you may hear the amplifier's faint hiss when it's quiet; it also uses a little battery. Checked at 30–69 % volume; at very low volumes it may not help (not measured). No WirePlumber change and no headset restart.
- Builds made on the current SteamOS beta (glibc 2.43) no longer need glibc 2.43 and start on stable SteamOS (glibc 2.39) again: the voice check's level used `log10f`, which glibc 2.43 versions anew. `scripts/package.sh` now stops when the binary needs a glibc newer than 2.38. Released versions up to 0.3.5 weren't affected.

## 0.3.5 (2026-10-31)

- Fix: with 0.3.4 on SteamOS 20261015 or later, when echo cancellation was on and the output wasn't the Frame's speakers, all sound to that output could stop while an app used the mic (seen with a USB-C audio adapter). 0.3.4 used Valve's fallback echo canceller for those outputs, and Valve puts its reference input in front of the current output; with a USB-C adapter that connection never started, so nothing played. Using only the fallback's mic side doesn't work either (the mic goes silent). The tracker now uses the main echo canceller for every output, as on SteamOS before 20261015, and never the fallback one, so automatic gain control is still applied only once. Its reference input stays on the Frame's speakers, so other outputs aren't touched. Takes effect after restarting the headset. 0.3.4 was marked as a pre-release and isn't offered as an update.

## 0.3.4 (2026-10-29)

- Fix: the mic was sometimes far too loud, especially in the first seconds after you start speaking. SteamOS 20261015 added a second ("fallback") echo canceller that Valve's microphone tracker uses instead of the main one when the output isn't the Frame's speakers. This project's tracker turned on every filter whose name starts with `echo_cancel`, so both were in the chain and their automatic gain control was applied twice (measured on a Frame: about 5 dB more at the start of speech and about seven times as many clipped samples). The tracker now follows Valve's choice: the main echo canceller with the Frame's speakers, the fallback one (with the current output as its echo reference) with any other output, never both (this could stop the sound on other outputs, seen with a USB-C audio adapter; fixed in 0.3.5). Noise suppression still follows its switch for every output (Valve only uses it with the Frame's speakers), and no filters are turned on when the default mic isn't the Frame's built-in mic, as the panel says. On SteamOS versions without the fallback (before 20261015), the tracker works exactly as in 0.3.3. Takes effect after restarting the headset.
- The header shows "In use" also while an app records from another mic set as the default. With the change above (on SteamOS 20261015 or later) no filters are turned on for that mic, and the panel used to tell "in use" only from the filters, so it said "Not in use".

## 0.3.3 (2026-10-25)

- Fix: noise suppression strength jumped back to SteamOS's default (23% / 500 ms) and that default was saved for the current output. With PipeWire 1.6 (seen on the current SteamOS beta), a value written to the noise suppressor before anything has used the mic since the headset started is kept by PipeWire and applied once the mic is used, but reading it back still shows the old value. The app took that old value for a change made in the panel. Now, when the value read right after writing is still the old one, the app shows and keeps the value it wrote, and goes back to what it reads once the reading changes (the mic is used, or the noise suppressor is set up again). If your saved strength was reset, set it once more after updating.

## 0.3.0 (2026-10-10)

- Simplified Chinese UI (thanks to @boholder and @dhies23, #1). The language switch at the bottom left is now **日本語 / English / 简体中文**. The panel starts in Simplified Chinese when Steam is set to Simplified Chinese (`schinese`; without a Steam setting, a `zh_CN` locale); the settings file and `--language` take `"sc"`.
- Mute: the panel notices when the default mic is muted, however it was muted (Steam, `wpctl set-mute`, an aux button shortcut, ...), by reading `wpctl get-volume @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SOURCE@` with the rest of the state. The header shows a red "Muted" with a crossed-out mic, and a **Mute** / **Unmute** button next to it (`wpctl set-mute @DEFAULT_AUDIO_SOURCE@ 1` / `0`, read back; no confirmation). The status and the button have fixed widths, so the button doesn't move when you mute. If muting or unmuting fails, the bottom line says so.
- Settings for each output: the Frame mic settings (echo cancellation, noise suppression and its strength) are remembered separately for each audio output (the Frame's speakers, USB-C earphones or adapters, Bluetooth headphones), keyed by the device's PipeWire node name (Valve's `alsa_loopback_device.*` wrapper maps to the speaker device itself; a Bluetooth device keeps one key across profiles). The app watches the default output every 2 seconds, also while the panel is closed (`pw-metadata -n default`, about 10 ms), and when it changes it applies that output's settings with the usual `wpctl settings --save` / `pw-cli set-param`, and the Quick tab says "<output> connected, so its saved settings are on" with **Undo**. It also applies them at startup. A new output starts from the Frame speakers preset if it's the Frame's speakers and from the Earphones preset otherwise; on the first start of this version, the current settings become the current output's. Changes made elsewhere (another tool, an aux button, `wpctl settings`) are remembered for the current output. Filter or virtual outputs are ignored.
- **Output** and **Mic** buttons above the settings: "Choose the output" lists the connected outputs and the ones used before, with **Play sound here** (`wpctl set-default`, the same setting as SteamVR's audio settings; Valve's wrapper node for the Frame's speakers), viewing and changing another output's settings ahead of time, and **Forget**. The list scrolls with the controller stick (`VROverlayFlags_SendVRSmoothScrollEvents`) or by dragging. "Mic to use" switches the default mic; with a mic other than the Frame's built-in one, the settings card explains that echo cancellation and noise suppression don't apply and offers **Use the built-in mic**.
- New look, matching the other Frame panels (shared `vendor/frame-ui/`): the settings card shows "When listening on <output>" with the two presets; the voice check history says which output (or external mic) each recording was made with; the bottom row has three languages and a new note line.
- **Apps & updates** (top right, with a pink dot when there's a new version or the companion app is missing): this app's version and the update check / **Update** (moved here from the full-width update card, which is gone), a **Check for updates** on/off switch (the `update_check` setting), the companion app frame-aux-shortcuts (toggles the mic mute with the aux button), and the list of the author's other Steam Frame apps (shared `vendor/frame-apps/`). With **Check for updates** on, the list and icons are fetched from frame.sasaken1102s.net at most once an hour. **Install** shows the exact installer command first and then opens it in Konsole.
- The Fine-tune sliders no longer have − / ＋ buttons (drag the slider).
- `--print` lists the outputs and mics with their keys and the node used for `set-default`, the defaults, and the saved settings for each output (read only). `--dump-png` has new `--view`, `--overlay`, `--fake-output`, `--fake-past-outputs`, `--fake-switched`, `--fake-external-mic`, `--fake-viewing`, `--preview-scroll`, `--fake-aux`, `--fake-apps-busy`, `--fake-apps-extra`, `--fake-confirm` and `--fake-launch` options; `--self-test` checks the output parsing with output captured on a Frame.

## 0.2.0 (2026-09-27)

First release with a prebuilt package (`frame-mic-tuner-<version>.tar.gz` + `SHA256SUMS`, built by `scripts/package.sh`); `install.sh` now installs either from that tarball or, as before, by building from a source checkout.

- Updates: an update card above the language/autostart/quit row always shows the running version. It asks GitHub for a newer release about once a day (`update_check` setting, on by default), and **Check now** looks right away regardless, even with automatic checking off. When a newer version is available, **Update** downloads and installs it after you confirm once (the card asks again with **Cancel** / **Update**; checksum-verified against the release's `SHA256SUMS`; nothing changes if that check fails). Shared with the other Frame apps via `vendor/frame-updater/`.
- The update card shows its state at a glance: an accent-colored border for a new version, the confirmation and a finished install, a red border with **Try again** / **Close** when an update failed, and red text (with **Check now**) when only the check failed.
- `install.sh` now writes `~/.config/frame-mic-tuner/install-args` with the options your last install used (currently just `--autostart`, when passed), so an update started from the panel reinstalls the same way.

## 0.1.0 (2026-09-27)

First release.

- A "Mic" panel in the SteamVR dashboard that switches the Steam Frame's microphone filters, with two tabs ("Quick" and "Fine-tune"; it reopens on the last one): presets for how you listen ("Earphones / headphones" = echo cancellation off and noise suppression off, "Frame speakers" = echo cancellation on and noise suppression off; each card shows what it sets, and both cards turn grey when the settings match neither), plus separate "Fine-tune" switches for echo cancellation and noise suppression. Changes apply at once, even while the mic is in use, and are kept after a restart.
- Noise filter strength sliders (Strictness = VAD threshold, Hold = VAD grace period) that apply live without restarting PipeWire. The values are saved and applied again whenever the app starts.
- Shows whether the mic is in use and which filters the sound actually goes through, read from the live PipeWire links.
- Voice check: record up to 10 seconds of what apps hear, keep the last 5 recordings in memory and play them back to compare settings. Nothing is written to disk.
- Japanese and English UI. It starts in the Steam Frame's language (from Steam's language setting) and remembers your choice. Colors meet WCAG 2.x AA contrast (`--contrast-report` prints every pair).
- Self-repair: if SteamVR loses the app's dashboard panel (for example when the dashboard restarts), the app notices within 3 seconds and recreates it.
- Starts from the dashboard's "+" (launch a program) list. Launching it again while it runs opens its panel. Optional autostart with SteamVR, switched from the panel.
- Includes the WirePlumber script (`contrib/wireplumber/`) that makes the filters switchable without restarting PipeWire.
- `install.sh` builds and installs everything into the home directory, with no sudo and no PipeWire / WirePlumber restart.
